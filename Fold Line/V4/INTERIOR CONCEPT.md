# Concept brief · the interior, round 1

Written 15 Oct 2026 with the creative-director skill, before the first demo was built. Sam picks from the reels, not from this file.

## Core concept

The map comes up off the desk. Each beat is the ground the map was printed from, standing up out of its own sheet, under the same lamp as the hero.

## What the interior refuses

- Layered paper mountains in a gradient with a sun disc and clouds. That is the stock "paper landscape" wallpaper, the first idea everyone has for this.
- Props on sticks: moon, stars, sun, signposts, flags on peaks, bunting.
- A daylight reset after the hero. The light changes when the beat changes, never because a section did.
- Invented ridgelines. Every silhouette in round 1 is measured: the view from 3.2 km up the Mangatepōpō valley, out of the New Zealand elevation model.
- A notebook card over the scene. The information is printed on the map.

## Shared ground, all three

- The real Tongariro map, laid out as a strip map the way John Ogilby drew roads in 1675. Each panel of the concertina is one straight stretch of the route with its own north arrow, so the L-shaped crossing reads left to right. Panel 1 is exactly the climb: the Mangatepōpō road end to South Crater. The track is the OpenStreetMap trace, the contours are every 20 m from the NZ elevation model, the labels and heights come from `treks-data.js`.
- The room stays dark. One warm key rakes in from the right front, at the azimuth of the hero's low sun at the end of the dive, so the cut lands in the light it left.
- The hiker is one plain silhouette. No face, no hat, no jacket colour.
- A caption per beat: a mono kicker, the beat's name in Instrument Serif with one word in the route red, the sourced line, the waypoints in mono.
- Practical information printed on the map: the kit list is the legend on the inside of the cover, season and permits are a red stamp in the margin.

## 01 Cut from the map

Origamic architecture (Masahiro Chatani's cut-and-fold cards). The skyline is cut out of the map itself and folds up on a hinge at its back edge. Each piece leaves its exact negative in the sheet in front of it, open to the dark bookcloth: Ngāuruhoe stands up and leaves a black cone lying on the map at its feet. Cream stock, the print shows through faintly on the back of each piece. The signature is the negatives.

## 02 Tone layers

A tunnel book of the same view cut into five distance bands, in five tones of basalt, matte card with a pale cut core. The light rakes in between the layers and each one throws its shadow on the next. The signature is small and real: the notch of Ngāuruhoe's summit crater on the cone layer.

## 03 White card

An architect's site model. Panel 1's ground grows out of the print as a contour stack, one off-white card every 40 m, the route draped over the terraces in red, the hiker a white card figure climbing them. The hero's Contour Stack at the scale of the walk. The signature is the hiker going up actual card edges on the Devil's Staircase.

## Risks, stated before building

- 01 and 02 share a silhouette. They have to differ in material and in what the sheet does, or Sam is choosing between two colours.
- 03 can read as more of the hero. It earns its place only if the walk over the terraces is legible.
- True angles make volcanoes look flat (Ngāuruhoe is 21 degrees above you from mid-valley). The standing pieces exaggerate height by 2, the model by 1.5, said plainly in the journal.
- No hut or shelter is placed in round 1. None of the researched points on the climb is a building, and nothing goes on the map that isn't sourced.
